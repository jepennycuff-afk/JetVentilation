💡 HFJV in 30 seconds

  • Tiny breaths, very fast: about 150 small pulses a minute instead of ~12 big breaths.
  • The target barely moves: lungs, diaphragm and liver stay nearly still, so IR can place needles without breath holds.
  • Why it matters: more accurate ablations and less radiation time.
  • CO₂ is watched through the skin (TcCO₂), because ETCO₂ isn't reliable on the jet.

🧭 A jet case at a glance

  1. 6:30 AM
    Text the IR anesthesia tech · confirm orientation · 2 PIVs
  2. Prep
    8.0 ETT + swivel adapter assembly (then set the adapter aside)
  3. Induce
    Intubate · TIVA
  4. On the table
    Attach TcCO₂ probe right away (2–10 min to calibrate) · note TcCO₂–ETCO₂ gap
  5. Connect
    Swivel adapter on · anesthesia machine to Manual, APL open, O₂ 1 LPM, vaporizer off
  6. Start jet
    Head to scanner: after the gantry is in · Feet to scanner: right away
  7. Stabilize
    Adjust DP until TcCO₂ is stable
  8. 🔒 Initial scan
    Settings locked from here. Changes only after Faculty discussion.
  9. Ablation
    Every 15 min: document TcCO₂, check TOF
  10. End
    Conventional vent · normalize ETCO₂ · 1–2 recruitment maneuvers · dock sensor · call tech

    ⚙️ Initial ventilator settings

    🌡️ CO₂ and oxygen

    Low SpO₂
    1. ↑ FiO₂ (OK at any time)
    2. Check breath sounds · suction the ETT
    3. Then, after Faculty discussion if post-scan: ↑ IT 5% · ↑ vent flow 0.5–1 LPM (creates PEEP) · ↑ DP 2 psi

    🚨 PP/PIP alarm

    ⚠ PP / PIP HIGH ALARM
    1. Jet tubing: kinked or blocked?
    2. ETT / airway: secretions? Suction.
    3. Paralysis: TOF ≤1/4? Redose if not.
    4. APL valve: fully open?
    5. Settings: DP / IT appropriate?
    6. Fixed: Silence, then press Alarm Reset

    💊 Paralysis

    • Check twitches every 15 minutes. Target ≤1 of 4 TOF.
    • Inadequate paralysis → movement, high pressures, poor ventilation.
    • Consider a paralytic infusion for long cases.

    🏁 End of case

    1. IR confirms the jet is no longer needed
    2. Switch to conventional ventilation (the swivel adapter can stay)
    3. Hyper- or hypoventilate to bring ETCO₂ back to baseline
    4. 1–2 recruitment maneuvers for atelectasis
    5. Return the TcCO₂ sensor to the Sentec docking compartment
    6. Ask the anesthesia tech to remove the equipment
